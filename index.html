The dataset is a snapshot of 303 characters from Wikipedia's Category:Marvel Comics superheroes, taken on 26 August 2026. An edge from A to B means the running text of A's Wikipedia article links to B's page. Not a navbox, not a sidebar — actual prose mentions. That distinction matters: template links would make every Avenger adjacent to every other Avenger, regardless of whether their articles actually reference each other.

Spider-Man pulls everything toward him

The single most striking feature of this network is Spider-Man's in-degree of 106 — meaning over a third of all 303 characters' articles link to his page. Hulk comes second at 64, then Wolverine at 60. The drop from first to second is almost as large as the drop from second to fifteenth. This is not a gradual ranking; it's a cliff with one character standing alone at the top.

The in-degree and out-degree top-10 lists are almost entirely different people. Spider-Man's in-degree of 106 makes him the character everyone mentions. But his out-degree is only 9 — his article is tightly focused. Betsy Braddock leads the out-degree ranking at 28: her long, sprawling article references X-Men, Captain Britain Corps, and Excalibur characters alike. She's the most prolific linker, not the most linked-to.

In-degree measures prominence: how many others reference you. Out-degree measures sprawl: how many others your article happens to mention. They're driven by different processes and produce different rankings. Mixing them into one undirected degree hides both stories.

The degree distributions

On a log–log plot, the in-degree distribution shows the characteristic heavy tail that network scientists look for. Most characters have very low in-degree (the median is 3), but the tail stretches to 106. The out-degree distribution is markedly more compressed: it tops out at 28, and the bulk of characters fall between 1 and 12. This asymmetry tells us the network has a few extremely prominent nodes but no correspondingly extreme "super-linker" — Wikipedia editors don't write articles that reference a hundred characters, even when those characters exist.

The islands

Seventeen characters sit completely alone — zero edges in either direction. These are the isolates, and they're worth a look. The giant weakly-connected component swallows 277 of 303 nodes. But between the giant and the isolates sits one genuine island: a cluster of 9 characters from the Strikeforce: Morituri series.

The Morituri island — 9 characters from a 1986 series, entirely disconnected from the main Marvel graph. Radian sits at the centre with degree 6.

The Morituri characters never crossed over into mainstream Marvel continuity, so no mainstream character's article mentions them. They only link to each other. This is exactly the kind of structure you'd miss by only looking at the giant component: it's a self-contained sub-community that exists because of editorial separation, not because the characters are obscure. They have full Wikipedia articles — they just never shared a storyline with Spider-Man.

The 17 true isolates are a different story. Characters like Baymax, Yo-Yo Rodriguez, and Solarman are categorised as Marvel superheroes on Wikipedia, but their articles apparently don't link to any other character in this 303-node set, and nobody links to them either. They exist in the category but not in the network. Some, like Baymax, are better known from adaptations than from comics; others, like Breeze Barton, are genuinely obscure Golden Age characters.

Reciprocity: who links back?

39.2% of edges are reciprocated — if A's article links to B, there's a roughly 4-in-10 chance that B's article links back to A. That's high enough to matter but far from universal. The unreciprocated edges tell a story about asymmetric prominence: Spider-Man's article doesn't link to the 97 characters whose articles link to him. He doesn't need to mention Enigma or NFL SuperPro; they need to mention him to establish their place in the universe.

In the strongly connected component analysis, 229 of the 303 characters can reach each other through directed paths in both directions — a large mutual core where information (or at least hyperlinks) flows in both directions. The remaining 74 characters can reach the core but can't be reached back, or sit entirely outside it.

What surprised us

The biggest surprise was the sheer asymmetry between the in-degree and out-degree rankings. We expected the same characters to dominate both — the famous ones would be mentioned often and would mention many others. Instead, the correlation is weak. Spider-Man is mentioned by a third of the network but mentions fewer than ten characters. Betsy Braddock, the top linker, doesn't crack the in-degree top 20. The network has a well-defined prominence hierarchy (measured by in-degree) and a separate editorial sprawl dimension (measured by out-degree), and the two are nearly orthogonal.

The second surprise was the Morituri island. We assumed the giant component would contain every non-isolated character. Finding a genuine 9-node archipelago from a single 1986 series — connected within itself but perfectly severed from 277 other characters — was a clean demonstration of how editorial firewalls create structural features in the network.
